Ph.D. dissertation

The Origin and Maintenance of Island Bird Species

Selection, Introgression, and Hybrid Incompatibilities in Myzomela Honeyeaters

Overview

Island systems make it possible to ask how evolutionary divergence is generated, eroded, and maintained. My dissertation approaches that problem from four connected directions in Myzomela honeyeaters.

01
Selection

Selection across island populations

I examine genomic signatures of selection to ask whether different island populations show distinct adaptive histories, and which genomic regions repeatedly emerge as candidates for local adaptation.

02
Introgression

Gene flow where species meet

I investigate how introgression contributes to genomic variation in sympatric populations, including whether gene flow can move locally advantageous alleles across species boundaries.

03
Hybrid incompatibilities

Why some genetic combinations fail

I study genomic patterns associated with asymmetric hybridization and reproductive incompatibility, with particular interest in mito-nuclear interactions and sex-linked components of isolation.

04
Extra-pair paternity

Mating behavior inside a hybrid zone

I ask whether extra-pair reproduction is associated with hybrid pairing, ancestry, genetic diversity, and local breeding structure, and whether extra-pair mating changes the genetic composition of offspring produced in the hybrid zone.
